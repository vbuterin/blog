# Chapter 3

Gladias was walking along a foot path through a local park. Tall trees were towering over him on both sides.

An audio program from Emerald, the local AI running from Gladias's hand device, was playing into his ear. At that particular moment, it was explaining the nuances of airborne virus transmission, and how the exact relationship between transmission rate and total city-wide cases could be computed and what that meant for the building design tax rubric rates.

To both his left and his right, there was a series of houses made out of large stone bricks, medieval in style but without any of the junk, dust on the ground, or other ugly imperfection that a real medieval structure would have had a thousand years earlier. Each house seemed to meld seamlessly into the trees and plants that surround it.

After about two hundred meters, the houses gave way to thick green shrubbery.

After about five minutes of that, the foot path started gently curving upward. Somehow both smoothly and suddenly, it transitioned into a sky bridge above a busy road, with many cars and autobuses and a few autonomous delivery vehicles passing under.

![Gladias: "Pause, I want to enjoy the view"], Gladias said to Emerald, out of habit whispering so softly that even his own hand device would likely not be able to make it what he said - but his silken cloth neck band picked it up perfectly.

Gladias stopped in the middle of the sky bridge, and turned to the side, putting his hands on the handrail. The handrail creaked slightly.

Gladias looked forward. The road beneath him continued for about two kilometers, and then turned right. Beyond that point, there was a mountain covered with trees. A few buildings, shaped like medieval castle towers, poked up above the trees - three of them were halfway up to the summit, and one on the very top.

Gladias's watch buzzed.

He took a look.

Seila was sending some photos from Freetown. The first was of the downtown of the city. The second was of a major park, taken from in front of an entrance gate. On the left, he could see a poster with some writing. Bringing it closer to his eyes, he could make out a price list.

He pressed a button, responding back with a heart symbol.

Gladias turned to once again face forward, and began to walk. After the sky bridge, the road continued, though with fewer trees and more buildings. After another two hundred meters, the road sloped down, and transitioned seamlessly into a tunnel, lit just enough that it's possible to comfortably see and read, but not more.

Gladias got to a Y-shaped intersection. Pasted on the wall, there was a poster, attached to the wall with four pieces of tape.

He stopped right in front of the sign to take a look. His watch and neck band automatically noticed that his mind was wandering, and the Dzego history lecture paused.

"Yay, more Arctic propaganda", Gladias thought. Of course, the Arctic Empire is free to speak just like everyone else, but he wondered what kind of Veridian would feel inclined to vandalize the public environment and put up a printout of their message in the Veridian tunnels. He started mulling over ways to rip the poster down.

Suddenly, Gladias felt a tap on his shoulder.

Gladias turned his face around.

The tunnel sloped up, and the outdoor light once again shone into Gladias's eyes. To his right, he saw a series of apartment buildings under construction, all covered with large tall barriers shielding the construction site from public view.

He remembered the sight - he had passed by there many months ago, and while he could not make out exactly how much had changed behind the barrier, it was clearly going more slowly than planned.

On the first barrier, he saw a poster reminding Veridians to be kind toward their families and their community.

On the second barrier, he saw a different kind of advertisement:

<blockquote>

Hydrogen hydroxide is:

* A commonly found substance in toxic sewage water
* Deadly in large doses
* Frequently administered to prisoners undergoing torture
* A source of skin damage if held for a prolonged time in its solid form

But did you know that you can buy YOUR OWN BOTTLE of fresh drinkable hydrogen hydroxide today?

Hydrafill, buy it now.

</blockquote>

On the right side of the advertisement, there was the classic Hydrafill water bottle, covered with yellow and green colors.

To his left, Gladias saw a long castle-like wall, mixing grey and purple colors. In front of the wall, there were two rows of trees, with a sidewalk in between them.

Then, a billboard:

"Learn about what Greater Plum Harbor is doing for your community!"

The text was in front of a background that featured robots picking up garbage, drawn in a child-friendly cartoon aesthetic. On the bottom right corner there was a two-dimensional barcode. Gladias scanned it out of curiosity.

![Borkat: "Have you ever been in there?"], the first man asked.

![Vossidem: "No I haven't, never managed to get a visa"], the second replied.

![Borkat: "It's a fascinating place, it looks totally different inside than the rest of Veridia, it's almost like a country inside a country."]

Gladias stopped, and also faced the billboard to avoid arousing suspicion. Overhearing a conversation was slightly impolite, but understanding public sentiment was part of his duty as a future Sentinel.

The second man looked down at the hand device that the two were sharing. ![Vossidem: "Oof, check what taxes they pay."]

![Gladias: "What taxes do they pay?"], Gladias asked Emerald, whispering so softly that only his neckband could make out what he is saying.

Gladias had not been inside Greater Plum Harbor for nearly a decade, so this made him all the more curious to see it again. He kept walking.

---

Gladias held his hand device near the marking on the entrance gate, and waited for one second.

"Proof verified, welcome", the machine said.

The buildings inside Greater Plum Harbor were more uniform than those outside. The trees were all of a few types, and carefully planted an equal distance apart on the sidewalk. Beyond having distinct names and distinct quarter-meter-wide logos, the stores and restaurants were not trying at all to distinguish themselves, instead accepting their visual role as small elements of the Greater Plum Harbor's whole. The buildings were noticeably more futuristic, more glass-and-steel, and more sharp-edges than the usual structures in Veridia.

After five minutes of walking, Gladias arrived at a tunnel entrance, and he walked down. Briefly, he went through a space where there were a large number of people walking.

At one moment, his watch beeped.

Two problems, Gladias thought.

First, too many people, not enough ventilation or filtration. Gladias pulled on a strap on his privacy robe's front face cover, tightening a belt that separated the area below his nose from the area above his nose.

Second, no idea what those unattested devices are. Here, there was no time to figure things out. Could be a kid's science project, could be surveillance. He increased his speed slightly, and kept walking.

After following his local AI's instructions and taking several turns through the maze, he was once again outside, this time inside of a hidden courtyard. There were several tables and chairs, with people eating and drinking tea. Gladias found one in a booth that had in a cloth protecting it. He went in, sat down, and took the hood off his privacy robe.

He tapped his hand device against a glowing circle in the middle of a table, and two seconds later heard a confirmation sound.

Two minutes later, a robot with a smiley face came in and put down tea on the table, along with a salad.

Five minutes later, another man wearing a privacy robe stepped in.
